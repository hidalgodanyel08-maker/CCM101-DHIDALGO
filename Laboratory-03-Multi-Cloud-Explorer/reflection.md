# Mission Reflection

## 1. Which cloud platform impressed you the most?

Google Cloud Platform stood out to me, mainly because of how they've built their infrastructure around containers and Kubernetes from the ground up. I've always been interested in container orchestration, and GCP's Anthos really caught my attention - the way you can manage workloads across different environments feels like a game-changer. Their AI and machine learning tools, especially Vertex AI, feel more polished and accessible compared to what I've seen elsewhere. The pricing model also seems more transparent, which I appreciate coming from a developer perspective where unexpected bills can be a nightmare.

## 2. What similarities exist among AWS, Azure, and GCP?

At their core, these three giants offer basically the same building blocks - you've got your compute options (EC2, VMs, Compute Engine), storage solutions (S3, Blob Storage, Cloud Storage), and database services across the board. They all have their own versions of managed Kubernetes, serverless functions, and content delivery networks. What really struck me is how they've all converged on similar naming conventions and service categories, probably because they're all trying to solve the same customer problems. Even their management consoles, despite looking different, follow similar patterns for organizing resources and setting up access controls. You can definitely tell they're all competing in the same space and borrowing good ideas from each other [citation:1][citation:8].

## 3. How do business requirements influence cloud platform selection?

This is where things get real interesting. Business needs aren't just a checkbox - they completely shape which cloud provider makes sense. I've seen situations where a company's entire decision came down to existing vendor relationships. For example, if an organization is already deep into Microsoft's ecosystem with Active Directory and Office 365, Azure becomes the path of least resistance. Meanwhile, I've worked with startups that jumped on AWS purely because of their generous free tier and startup programs that give them credits to get off the ground. And then there are data-heavy companies doing serious machine learning work - they naturally gravitate toward GCP because Google's been living and breathing AI research for years. At the end of the day, it's not about which cloud is "best" in some abstract sense, but which one fits the specific puzzle pieces of budget, existing tech stack, regulatory requirements, and team expertise.

## 4. What did you learn from acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant?

Taking on the consultant role really shifted how I think about cloud technology. It's way too easy to get excited about shiny technical features, but I learned pretty quickly that what actually matters is solving business problems. I had to force myself to step back and think about things like total cost of ownership, operational overhead, and whether a particular solution would actually make life easier for the people who'd have to maintain it. The exercise taught me to ask better questions upfront - things like "what's your team's experience level?" or "how quickly do you need to scale?" - instead of jumping straight into technical comparisons. I also realized that presenting recommendations clearly and backing them up with concrete reasoning is half the battle in getting buy-in from stakeholders who aren't as technically inclined.

My GitHub portfolio definitely looks more polished now.
